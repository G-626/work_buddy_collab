## Basics [open]

- Age: 15 | Year: S4 (mainstream placement, EDB Tier 2 support)
- Reading level: at age level; strong vocabulary; prefers written over verbal instructions
- Voice preference: third person — finds first-person materials childish and exposing
- Language: English at school; Cantonese and English at home (parent updates: plain English)

## Communication profile [open]

- Highly articulate in structured settings (class discussions, written work) but goes quiet
  in unstructured social settings (group work, lunch, corridors).
- Masks heavily: copies peers' behaviour to blend in, then exhausts. Teachers report she
  "seems fine" — parents report meltdowns at home after school.
- Will not ask for help in front of peers — perceives it as exposure. Asks questions by
  email or after class instead.
- Sensitive to perceived criticism: a neutral correction ("try again") can be heard as
  failure. Needs feedback framed as process, not judgement.

## Triggers & sensory [open]

- **Being singled out in front of peers** — praise or correction in front of the class
  causes visible distress, then withdrawal. Primary anxiety source.
- **Unstructured social time** (group work without assigned roles, lunch seating) — drifts
  to edges, eats alone, reports "I don't know where to sit."
- **Ambiguous instructions** ("be creative", "work together") — freezes, produces nothing,
  then feels she has failed.
- No sensory issues. No noise, light, or touch sensitivities reported.

## What works [open]

- *Session note: Fact-checker role in the group poster kept engagement without prompts; written instructions removed ambiguity.*

- **Private feedback** — written comments on work, or a quiet word after class, never in
  front of peers. Responds well, asks follow-up questions.
- **Assigned roles in group work** — "You are the note-taker" works; "work together" does not.
- **Advance warning of changes** — even small ones (room change, supply teacher). A note in
  her planner or a message the day before prevents escalation.
- **Art as regulation** — sketching in a notebook calms her. Teachers allow this during
  listening tasks; it does not mean she isn't paying attention.

## Special interests [open]

- **Visual art** — draws constantly; strong observational skill; uses drawing to process
  emotions. Art teacher reports she is "the most skilled student in the year."
- **Graphic novels** — reads and writes them; interested in visual storytelling structure.
- Likes systems and rules in creative work (colour theory, perspective, narrative structure)
  — not chaos.

## Notes for materials [open]

- Third person only. Never "I" statements — she finds them childish and exposing.
- No cartoonish layouts, no "well done!" framing, no star charts. She notices and
  feels patronised.
- Frame challenges as shared ("many people find group work hard") not personal
  ("Priya finds group work hard").
- Written, not verbal. She reads; she does not absorb spoken instructions.
