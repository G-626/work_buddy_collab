# Parent Report Template

Generate ONE Parent Report following every rule below. Rules marked ⚠️ are also
enforced by the linter (see `linter-parent-guide.md`). Write like a therapist who
respects the family: warm, structured, honest, and practical.

## 1. Audience

A parent or caregiver who knows their child deeply but may not know professional
vocabulary. They are worried, busy, and want to help. This report is their window
into what happens in sessions — they should feel informed and equipped, never
lectured or alarmed.

## 2. Purpose

Give the parent five things: (1) what happened in the session, (2) how their child is
progressing toward their goals (with evidence, not vague reassurance), (3) what to
reinforce at home, (4) what to say (consistent with what the teacher and the child
hear), and (5) what the next step is. The parent should close the report knowing
exactly what to do before the next session.

## 3. Source-of-truth rule (⚠️ linter-enforced)

**Only `[open]` passport sections may be used.** No `[teacher]`/`[parent]` audience
tags exist — sensitivity tags are the control mechanism. Anything `[team]` or
`[clinical]` does not belong here. If a fact is needed but only exists in a restricted
section, flag it in the linter report rather than leaking it.

## 4. Language rules (⚠️ all linter-enforced)

- **Warm but not condescending.** The parent is a partner, not a client. Avoid
  clinical distance ("the student exhibits…") and avoid over-familiarity
  ("your little one…").
- **Plain language.** No jargon. If a professional term is used, define it in
  brackets. Aim for ~age-12 reading level unless the passport specifies otherwise.
- **Short sentences.** One idea per sentence. Bullets over paragraphs.
- **Positive framing.** ⚠️ Say what TO do, not what NOT to do. ("If she wants to
  skip an event, try asking what specifically she's worried about" — not "Don't let
  her avoid social events.")
- **No diagnosis language.** Never mention autism, ASD, or any label. The parent
  knows their child's diagnosis — the report doesn't repeat it.
- **Progress is evidence-based.** Every "went well" claim is anchored to a count,
  quote, or observable from the session.

## 5. Structure

1. **Header**: Student name, date, session/situation (one line). Plus:
   *"Draft for professional review — generated by CaseCraft."*
2. **Overview** (2–3 sentences): What the student is preparing for or what the
   session covered, in plain language. Grounded in the situation brief.
3. **What we're working on** (table, 2–4 rows): For each current goal relevant to
   the session:
   | What we're working on | Evidence this session | Status |
   |---|---|---|
   | (goal text, plain language) | (counts, quotes, observables) | Progressing / Met / Needs attention |
   ⚠️ Status is a professional judgement the parent can verify from the evidence
   column — no status without evidence. Never use the words "goal" or "progress" in
   a way that implies clinical assessment; the framing stays "what we're working on".
4. **What went well** (3–5 bullets): Positive, observable highlights from the
   session — things the parent would want to hear and reinforce. Positive-first
   framing throughout.
5. **Home strategies** (3–5 bullets): Specific, home-feasible actions drawn from the
   passport's "What works" section. One action, one sentence, one bullet. Each must
   be something a parent can actually do at home (practice scripts, adjust routines,
   prepare in advance).
6. **Same words at home** (2–3 examples): The exact phrases the child is hearing at
   school, so the parent can use the identical words at home. ⚠️ These must be
   **word-for-word identical** to the Teacher Guide and Social Story (if generated).
7. **What to watch for** (2–3 bullets): Observable signs the student is finding the
   situation hard, and what the parent can do if they see them.
8. **Next session focus** (1–2 lines): What the next session will work toward —
   keeps the parent oriented and gives them something to reinforce in between.
9. **Contact** (1 line): The professional's name and role. Leave blank for the
   reviewer to fill.

## 6. Length

1–2 pages. Structure over word count: no section may balloon, every section earns its
place. A report that respects the parent's time gets read.

## 7. Personalisation rules

- **Special interests**: mention as a connection point ("You could look at the route
  map together") — never as a reward ("If he does well, he can play with trains").
- **Triggers**: home-relevant triggers only. School-only triggers (e.g. being
  singled out in class) belong in the teacher guide, not here — unless they have a
  home parallel.
- **What works**: home-feasible strategies only.

## 8. Uncertainty rule

If a fact is unknown (e.g. whether the family has visited the venue before), either
phrase it as typical ("Many families find it helpful to visit a new place before the
first day") or omit it and flag the question in the linter report.
⚠️ Never assert an unstated fact.
