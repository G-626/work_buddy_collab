# Parent Guide Template

Generate ONE Parent Guide following every rule below. Rules marked ⚠️ are also enforced by
the linter (see `linter-parent-guide.md`).

## 1. Audience

A parent or caregiver who knows their child well but may not know the professional
vocabulary. They are worried, busy, and want to help. Write for someone who loves this
student and needs practical guidance, not clinical explanation.

## 2. Purpose

Give the parent three things: (1) what the student is preparing for, (2) what to reinforce
at home, (3) what language to use (consistent with what the teacher and the student
are hearing). The parent should feel informed, not lectured.

## 3. Language rules (⚠️ all linter-enforced)

- **Warm but not condescending.** The parent is a partner, not a client. Avoid clinical
  distance ("the student exhibits…") and avoid over-familiarity ("your little one…").
- **Plain language.** No jargon. If a professional term is used, define it in brackets.
  Reading level: aim for age ~12 reading level unless the dossier specifies otherwise.
- **Short sentences.** One idea per sentence. Bullet points over paragraphs.
- **Positive framing.** ⚠️ Say what TO do, not what NOT to do. ("Don't let her avoid
  social events" → "If she wants to skip an event, try asking what specifically she's
  worried about.")
- **No diagnosis language.** Never mention autism, ASD, or any label. The parent knows
  their child's diagnosis — the guide doesn't need to repeat it.

## 4. Structure

1. **Header**: Student name, date, situation (one line).
2. **What's happening** (2–3 sentences): What the student is preparing for, in plain
   language. Grounded in the situation brief.
3. **What you can do at home** (3–5 bullets): Specific, home-feasible actions. Drawn from
   the dossier's `[parent]`-tagged sections. Each bullet is one action, one sentence.
4. **What to say** (2–3 examples): Exact phrases the parent can use, drawn from the
   dossier's communication profile and coping scripts. ⚠️ These phrases must be identical
   to those in the Teacher Guide and Social Story (if generated).
5. **What to watch for** (2–3 bullets): Signs the student is finding the situation hard,
   and what the parent can do if they see them.
6. **Who to contact** (1 line): The professional's name and role. Leave blank for the
   reviewer to fill.

## 5. Personalisation rules (from the dossier)

- Only use sections tagged `[parent]` or `[teacher, parent]` or `[teacher, parent, employer]`.
- **Special interests**: mention as a connection point ("You could look at the route map
  together") not as a reward ("If he does well, he can play with trains").
- **Triggers**: include home-relevant triggers. School-specific triggers (e.g. being
  singled out in class) do not belong here unless they have a home parallel.
- **What works**: prioritise strategies that are feasible at home (a parent cannot
  restructure the school day, but can practice scripts, adjust routines, or prepare
  the student in advance).

## 6. Length

150–300 words. Shorter than the Teacher Guide — parents are busier and more emotionally
invested. If it doesn't fit on half a page, it's too long.

## 7. Uncertainty rule

If a fact is unknown (e.g. whether the family has visited the venue before), either
phrase it as typical ("Many families find it helpful to visit a new place before the
first day") or omit it and flag the question in the linter report. ⚠️ Never assert an
unstated fact.
