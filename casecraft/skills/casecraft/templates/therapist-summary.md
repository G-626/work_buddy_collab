# Therapist Summary Template

Generate ONE Therapist Summary following every rule below. This is the working
document for the professional team — the one place professional vocabulary and
clinical-scope detail are expected and safe.

## 1. Audience

The professional team: the school social worker, therapist, and SEN coordinator.
They know the student, know the goals, and need the working detail the family-facing
documents deliberately omit. They will use this to plan the next session and to brief
each other.

## 2. Purpose

Give the team the full picture from this situation: the relevant passport detail
(`[open]` + `[team]`), goal progress with evidence, patterns, and open questions.
This is the "bigger picture" document the Parent Report points to.

## 3. Source-of-truth rule (⚠️ linter-enforced)

**`[open]` + `[team]` sections only. `[clinical]` is never included.** The
`[clinical]` section (diagnosis, medication, clinical records) is reserved for the
professional's own records and is never rendered into any generated view — including
this one. If `[team]` content is missing or thin, say so and flag it.

## 4. Language rules

- **Professional but precise.** Clinical-scope vocabulary is allowed (regulation,
  sensory profile, goal attainment) — define only where a colleague from another
  discipline might not know the term.
- **Observables carry the argument.** Every claim about progress cites counts,
  quotes, or timestamps from the session. No "I feel he improved" without evidence.
- **Positive-first still applies.** Findings are framed as support and reinforcement;
  difficulties are neutral observables plus questions, never a verdict on the child.
- **No diagnosis language.** Even here, do not assert diagnoses. The `[clinical]`
  section exists for that; this document supports, it does not diagnose.

## 5. Structure

1. **Header**: Student name, date, situation (one line), and the professional's name
   (from the session/brief context). Plus: *"Working document for the professional
   team — not for family distribution."*
2. **Relevant passport summary** (short): The `[open]` + `[team]` facts that matter
   for this situation — communication profile, triggers, what-works, current goals.
   Condensed, not repeated verbatim.
3. **Goal progress** (table): For each relevant goal — goal text, evidence from this
   situation (counts/quotes/observables), progress status, recommended next target.
   This table may be more clinical than the Parent Report's version (e.g. prompt
   counts, escalation markers) but must never contradict it.
4. **Patterns & trends** (if applicable): Aggregated movement-event counts aligned to
   context (what activity, sounds/transitions, time of day), from the passport's
   Patterns section. Descriptive and correlational; surprising patterns are questions,
   not conclusions.
5. **Session log reference**: The relevant session(s) this summary draws from.
6. **Open questions for the team** (2–5): Anything ambiguous, missing, or needing a
   professional decision (e.g. "confirm whether the 09:22 pause correlated with the
   mixer onset across sessions").
7. **Recommended next step** (1–2 lines): A concrete suggestion for the next session
   or the next stakeholder action.

## 6. Length

1–2 pages. Dense but structured — the team reads this to brief each other, not to
admire prose.

## 7. Uncertainty rule

If a fact is unknown or a pattern is ambiguous, say so and route it as a question.
⚠️ Never assert an unstated fact; never upgrade correlation to causation.
