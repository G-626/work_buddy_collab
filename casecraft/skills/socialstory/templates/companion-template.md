# Companion One-Pager Template (for staff/parents)

Purpose: adults get a short briefing sheet so the story is USED correctly. A Social Story
only works if the adults around the student present it consistently.

## Header

- Student: `<name>` | Date: `<date>` | Story file: `<path>`
- Situation: `<one-line summary>`
- Prepared by: `<professional name/role>` (leave blank for reviewer to fill)

## What this story is for

Two sentences: what the story describes and why it was prepared now (e.g. upcoming event).
State plainly: this is a description to build understanding, not a behaviour contract.

## How to use it

1. Read it together with the student in a calm moment, **before** the event
   (ideally 2–7 days ahead; repeat the day before).
2. Let the student ask questions; answer with the story's words where possible.
3. Keep a printed copy accessible on the day (bag, phone photo).
4. Afterwards, note what matched and what differed — feed corrections back for the next story.

## What the story deliberately does NOT do

- It does not instruct or grade behaviour.
- It does not mention diagnosis or frame the student as a problem.
- Coaching sentences are intentionally few — please do not add "don't" rules when reading.

## Evidence note

Social Stories are an established evidence-based practice, most effective when individually
tailored, read in advance, and used consistently across settings. Poorly written stories
(too directive, too vague) are ineffective — if you edit the story, keep the descriptive
tone and avoid adding instructions.
