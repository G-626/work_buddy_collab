# Story Generation Template

Generate ONE story following every rule below. Rules marked ⚠️ are also enforced by the linter.

## 1. Title

Literal and factual, not cute. Format: `<What happens>` — e.g. "My First Day at Sunbeam Bakery".

## 2. Sentence types (Carol Gray Social Stories 10.2)

Use these sentence types, in this proportion:

| Type | Purpose | Target share | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Descriptive** | State facts: what, where, when, who, why people do things | majority (≥ 60%) | "The bakery opens at 9:00." "Mrs. Chan is the supervisor. Supervisors help workers know what to do." |
| **Coaching** | Gently guide a response; soft verbs only ("can try", "might", "one option is") | minority (≤ 1 per 3 descriptive) | "If I am not sure where to stand, I can ask Mrs. Chan, 'Where should I stand?'" |
| **Perspective** | What others know/feel/expect | occasional | "Mrs. Chan expects workers to wash their hands before touching food. This keeps food safe." |
| **Affirmative** | Reassure; state a shared value or certainty | occasional | "It is okay to ask questions. Most people ask questions on their first day." |
| **Partial** | Sentence stem the student completes with the professional | optional, max 1–2 | "When I feel unsure, I can ______." |

⚠️ Ratio rule: at most **1 coaching sentence per 3 descriptive sentences**. When in doubt,
describe — do not instruct.

## 3. Language rules (⚠️ all linter-enforced)

- **Literal and concrete.** No idioms, metaphors, sarcasm, exaggeration. ("hit the ground
  running" → "start working")
- **Precise time and quantity.** ⚠️ Banned vague words: *soon, later, maybe, in a bit, a
  while, a lot, often, sometimes* (unless describing genuine variability, then say so plainly:
  "Some days the bakery is busy. Some days it is quiet.").
- **No double negatives.** ("It is not uncommon…" → "Many people…")
- **Positive framing.** ⚠️ Say what TO do, not what NOT to do. ("Don't shout" → "I can use a
  quiet voice inside."). Never name the student's feared behaviour as the headline.
- **First person** ("I…") unless `voice=third` (then use the student's name; this is the
  "social article" register for older teens who dislike first-person childish formats).
- **Reading level:** write at or below the dossier's `reading_level`. Short sentences.
  One idea per sentence.

## 4. Adolescent register ⚠️

- No babyish framing, no cartoon narration, no moral-of-the-story cheerfulness.
- Calm, factual, respectful tone — like a well-written briefing note for a colleague.
- Length: 150–300 words for a one-pager unless the situation genuinely needs more.
- The student is never the problem. Situations are new or complex; other people find them
  hard too — say so when true.

## 5. Personalisation rules (from the dossier)

- **Special interests:** use as *anchor* or *motivator*, never as decoration or reward-bait.
  E.g. a transit interest → include the actual route details; a baking interest → name the
  products accurately.
- **Triggers/sensory flags:** if the situation plausibly involves a flagged trigger
  (e.g. noise), include an honest descriptive sentence about it + one coaching sentence with
  a concrete coping option from the dossier (e.g. ear defenders, asking for a break).
  Do not catastrophise; state facts.
- **Literal-thinking flag:** double-check every sentence for implied meaning.
- **Known successful strategies:** prefer coping options listed in the dossier over generic ones.

## 6. Structure

1. **Title**
2. **Opening (2–4 sentences):** what is happening, when, where — pure description.
3. **Middle (bulk):** descriptive walk-through in chronological order of what the student
   will see/do; weave in perspective and affirmative sentences; insert coaching sentences
   only at genuine decision/uncertainty points.
4. **Closing (1–2 sentences):** what happens at the end + one affirming sentence.
5. **Optional 1–2 partial sentences** for the professional to complete with the student.

## 7. Uncertainty rule

If a fact is unknown (e.g. whether there is a lunch break), either phrase it as typical
("Most workplaces have a lunch break. I can ask Mrs. Chan about breaks.") or omit it and
flag the question in the linter report. ⚠️ Never assert an unstated fact.
